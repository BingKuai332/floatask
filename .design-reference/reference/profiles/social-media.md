# Social Media Content

The following is a general reference for this genre; user requirements, attachments, and the actual scenario take precedence.

Applies to Xiaohongshu covers, video covers, WeChat Official Account covers, Moments or Instagram posts, and multi-page carousel content.

**Benchmarks**: mature content systems published by media outlets and brands on platforms such as Xiaohongshu, Instagram, and WeChat Official Accounts, as well as social media work by brands like Nike, Apple, and Spotify that balances feed recognizability, content distribution, and series consistency.

## Goals

### Adapt to the platform and browsing scenario first
Confirm the platform, aspect ratio, cover task, and publishing format first. A feed cover must make its subject quickly recognizable at thumbnail size; a WeChat Official Account cover must account for cropping areas; carousel content must suit page-by-page swiping. Do not mechanically scale everything to the canvas size.

### Use the cover to build reading motivation
The cover focuses on one key message, person, or visual tension; do not shrink the entire body content into the first screen. The title, imagery, and content value proposition should form a direct relationship, so users know why it is worth reading on.

### Give series content unified identity with page-level variation
Multi-page content should share fonts, color palettes, core graphics, or image treatment, while varying the composition across the opening, explanation, comparison, case-study, and closing pages. Unified does not mean copying the same set of cards onto every page.

### Complete the work by combining image assets with layout editing
Content about people, places, products, and lifestyle usually requires searching for real images; fictional concepts, moods, and custom illustrations can be generated, then unified visually through cropping, background removal, color grading, and compositing. Set titles, body text, page numbers, and calls to action as independent typography in `pptd.md`; avoid generating text that must be displayed accurately inside images.

## Visual references

The following directions are designed for feed thumbnails, page-by-page swiping, and series operations — not for shrinking a poster. First decide how the cover makes people stop, how the inner pages advance the information, and how the final page wraps up or drives action; then choose one visual system to run through the entire set.

The following entries only help illustrate what good design in this genre can look like. They are not a routing table to be matched item by item, and you are not required to fully adopt any single one. Form your own direction from the user's content, then borrow composition, typography, color, imagery, or material treatment from these entries as needed; reasonable approaches not listed here are also acceptable.

### 01. Hook-title cover

**Applies to**: opinion pieces, how-to guides, knowledge summaries, video covers, and content with a strong conclusion. Keep only one core hook of 8–18 characters on the cover, set as 2–4 lines of large type occupying most of the area, and use a number, a keyword color, or a small subject image to create a second focal point; the account name, column name, and issue number recede to the edges. **Production**: first check at platform thumbnail size that the cover is still readable, then lay out the inner pages; do not cram the entire body summary onto the cover, and do not create hierarchy with five or six different text styles.

### 02. Magazine-style portrait carousel

**Applies to**: interviews, brand stories, portfolios, fashion, and lifestyle features. The cover uses one high-quality portrait or work image with a short title; inner pages alternate between full-bleed images, half-image half-text spreads, quote pages, and detail pages; unify the cropping tone, color grading, page numbers, and headline font to create a continuous flipping experience. **Production**: prioritize real assets for people and keep the same person consistent; place body text in quiet areas; do not apply the same frame to every page, and do not overlay small text on faces.

### 03. Product knowledge breakdown card set

**Applies to**: new product introductions, ingredient explanations, buying guides, usage tutorials, and brand education. The cover pairs the product with one clear benefit; subsequent pages unfold along "what it is — why — how to use it — who it suits — what to watch out for"; each page addresses only one question, using close-ups, annotations, or comparison images, rather than stacking five selling points evenly. **Production**: reuse product images from the same baseline and unify the background removal; set specifications and copy as independent typography; do not alter the product, and do not add effects that were not provided.

### 04. Lifestyle photo diary

**Applies to**: travel, restaurant and shop visits, city walks, daily records, books/film/music, and behind-the-scenes content. Build each page from 1–3 photos connected by time and place, paired with short phrases, dates, ticket stubs, or handwritten marks, so the whole set reads like a continuous diary; the cover keeps the most emotional image, and the inner pages progress through wide shots, people, details, and a closing shot. **Production**: prioritize the user's photos or real searched assets, and unify color temperature and grain; collages must revolve around the same event and must not become a random sticker wall.

### 05. Hand-drawn explainer carousel

**Applies to**: science popularization, psychology, education, parenting, public welfare, and introductions to complex concepts. Use one fixed narrator character or a set of objects in the same line weight to run through the series; the cover poses the question, and inner pages answer it through arrows, metaphorical scenes, and step-by-step reveals; use a paper-white background with 2–4 stable colors, and keep body text in a clear print typeface. **Production**: write the page-by-page script before generating/drawing illustrations; the character's appearance must stay consistent; the hand-drawn style cannot replace facts and page hierarchy.

### 06. Rankings, comparisons, and checklists

**Applies to**: recommendation lists, before-and-after comparisons, pitfall-avoidance guides, step-by-step checklists, and quarterly reviews. The cover highlights the list's theme or one contrastive conclusion; inner pages use fixed numbering, a common baseline, and consistent metrics to support quick comparison; key items may be enlarged, but keep 1–3 objects per page, and end with selection criteria or a summary. **Production**: unify the scale and background of object images; rankings and data must have sources; do not mislead about relative merit with inconsistent scales, and do not squeeze ten items onto one page.

### 07. Pop culture and internet nostalgia

**Applies to**: music, entertainment, trends, gaming, retro internet, and content aimed at young users. Use flash-lit portraits, old windows, pixel icons, low-resolution textures, and a set of high-signal colors to create cover impact, while inner pages return to readability with regular grids and quieter backgrounds; retro elements must match the era of the subject. **Production**: set accurate text and page numbers as independent typography, and concentrate special effects on the cover and transition pages; do not turn the entire set into an unreadable screenshot of an old webpage.

### 08. Brand recurring-column system

**Applies to**: long-running weekly digests, knowledge columns, store new arrivals, podcasts, and serialized opinion pieces. Fix the column name, issue-number position, fonts, color roles, and one variable image window; change the main image or theme color on each issue's cover; keep 3–5 page masters for the inner pages, rotating among opening, argument, case study, data, and closing. **Production**: first design at least three issues' covers side by side to verify recognizability and differentiation, then expand the inner pages; a fixed system does not mean copying the same composition every issue, and do not lose brand assets by chasing trends.

### 09. Triptych travel and video covers

**Applies to**: travel vlogs, city guides, restaurant and shop visits, video columns, and location recommendations. Use three vertical photos carrying the environment, the person, and the detail respectively, keeping the same color temperature and separating them with narrow gaps; one short place name or theme word spans two to three panels, while the subtitle, date, and hand-drawn route marks stay very small. **Production**: prioritize real assets from the same trip or the same location, and check the main title and the person at thumbnail size; do not stitch three unrelated stock photos together to fake a "travel feel."

### 10. Mobile long-image diary

**Applies to**: outdoor records, event recaps, long-form guides, training logs, and continuous process sharing. Break the content into numbered sections following the phone's scrolling rhythm, alternating dark or paper backgrounds, cutout action photos, diagonal labels, route arrows, and checklist pages; the cover gives the theme and the main image, the middle breathes between large images and high-density information, and the ending delivers a clear summary or call to action. **Production**: plan the full scrolling rhythm first, then reuse the same fonts, spot colors, and photo treatment; do not stretch a single poster indefinitely, and do not make every section equally crowded.

## Prohibited items
1. Do not ignore platform aspect ratios, thumbnail readability, and safe cropping areas.
2. Do not pack all information evenly into identical cards, leaving the cover without a focus and the inner pages without rhythm.
3. Do not increase the mobile reading burden with overly long titles, overly small font sizes, and low-contrast text.
4. Do not allow multi-page content to contradict itself in people, products, image color grading, or visual motifs.
